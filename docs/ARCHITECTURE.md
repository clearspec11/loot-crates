# Loot Crates: how it works

The developer's map of the module: how each game runs on the one core, what the data records hold, and where each script sits. The player and GM guide is the [README](../README.md); the design tokens and rules are in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Overview

A Foundry VTT module for dnd5e. The GM builds **pools** of items (dragged in from any compendium, the Items sidebar or another module's imports, or bulk-added by filter), prices **boxes** that draw from those pools at chosen rarity odds, and players buy a box with their character's coins. The pull plays as a crate-opening spin that everyone at the table watches, and the item lands in the character's inventory.

- Players: the **Crates** button at the top of the Items sidebar opens the shop.
- GM: **Manage crates** beside it opens the manager: pools, boxes, and the pull log with refunds (so does **Game Settings, Loot Crates**). The manager's **View shop** opens the shop, and the shop's header controls hold **Manage crates** for the way back.
- Prices are typed the way the shop shows them, "50 gp", "5 sp" or "12 gp 5 sp" (a bare number is gold), in each Boxes row's price field (saved on Enter or Tab) and in the editors.
- Purchases go over the module socket to the active GM's client, which rolls, deducts, grants and broadcasts the reveal. No GM online means the shop is closed.

## Games of chance

Every game the module runs, the crate first, the Wishing Well, the dice and the deck, is a record in the `boxes` setting with a `kind` (a record with none is a crate, so nothing written before kinds existed changes). The core is one copy for all of them: the money path on the GM (`pull.js`: the guards, the roll, the charge and any payout through one `settle()`, the grant with its rollback), the request, queue and broadcast (`socket.js`), the Pull log row and the refund (`refund.js`: coins back, payout taken back, item removed, stock and state restored, card marked), and the hold until the spin lands (`reveal.js`: the sealed chat card, the hidden sheet row, the held shop odds). A kind (`kinds.js` has the contract) supplies what differs: the roll, the reveal's animation and result block, the chat card and the shop card. A roll may end in no item (a miss) or in coins paid out: log rows carry `payoutCp` (rows from before it count as 0), and a refund that would have to take back winnings the character has spent is refused, naming the shortfall. Another module adds a game with `game.modules.get("loot-crates").api.registerKind`.

**Dragon's Teeth** (`dicegame.js`) is the tavern dice game, one variant for now: "beat the dragon". The GM sets the stake, the dice (2d6 or 3d6), the dragon's number and the payout multiplier, and can let a perfect roll (every die on its top face) win an item from the game's pools too. A player bets the stake; the GM's client rolls Foundry's own dice (`new Roll(formula).evaluate({ allowInteractive: false })`, inside the write queue, so a GM on manual dice entry is never prompted mid-purchase); a total over the dragon's number wins the stake times the multiplier through the same `settle()`, a total equal to it loses. The faces are decided and logged before any client shows a die, and travel in the reveal as the serialised Roll, so every client shows the same dice; no ChatMessage ever carries the Roll (that would print the total everywhere the moment it was created). The reveal is the dragon's number over three dice that tumble and settle left to right, then the total and the verdict in the live region ("15 beats the dragon's 12 · Rolled 4, 6, 5 on 3d6 · Won 30 gp · 1020 gp in the purse"); with Dice So Nice active the window waits for its 3D roll instead and lands with it (a ~6 s fallback covers a stalled animation). A win posts a card with the faces and the verdict, sealed like a crate's until this client's dice land; a loss is quiet, with a word for the buyer alone. The shop card says the rule and the exact odds ("Win 26%, Lose 74%" for 3d6 over 12), what a win pays and whether a perfect roll wins an item, and refuses a dragon the dice can't beat. A refund takes the winnings back less the stake in one move; a refund the purse can't cover (the winnings spent) is refused with the shortfall named, never minted.

The **Deck of Many Things** (`deck.js`) is one card drawn from a deck the GM writes in the manager (New deck): each card is a name, what it does (an item from the deck's pools at rarity odds, coins paid out, coins taken, an effect whose text the GM plays out, or a blank), a weight and whether it is drawn once per deck. Nothing copyrighted ships; the five default cards are placeholders. A once card drawn leaves the deck until the GM presses Reshuffle, read from the pull log the way the well's cap is, so a refund puts the card back by itself. The price rises with each draw in a row (the first draw at the record's price, then base × step, base × step², as long as each draw follows the last within the deck's window: two minutes unless the GM sets another in the deck editor, "Draws count as in a row within N minutes", where blank or 0 makes every draw a first draw; the window is stored on the deck, and a deck saved before it reads two minutes), charged by the core (`pull.js` takes the kind's price of a play) and written to the row, so a refund gives back what was paid and takes the step back. A loss card takes its gold through the same one `settle()` as a negative payout, never more than the purse holds after the stake. The reveal is one card that lifts, holds face-down under three soft ticks, then turns; every draw posts a sealed chat card (an effect's text in it, for the GM); the buyer alone gets **Draw again for N gp** beside Close, which sends the next draw as an ordinary pull and closes the window once the next has opened. A loss is the one thing in the module that means "you lost something": a cold slate token (`--lc-bane`), a dimmed vault and two falling notes.

The **Wishing Well** (`well.js`) is the quiet game: a copper or silver thrown in, almost always nothing, now and then the coin back, rarely an item from its pools at rarity odds, with the crate's pity rule counting throws. Every throw writes a log row, so every throw is refundable; the Pull log folds a character's misses for a day into one line ("5 throws, nothing") with Refund one and a Hide misses switch; only an item posts a chat card. Each character may throw N times per in-game day: the day is the world's calendar (`game.time.components`, `clock.js`) while dnd5e's calendar is on. With it off (dnd5e 6's default, where a long rest doesn't move the clock) a day ends at each character's long rest, the way dnd5e recovers daily uses: `dnd5e.restCompleted` with `newDay` reaches the active GM (a player's client sends a `newDay` socket message, counted only for a sender who owns the character), which counts it in the actor-state setting's `days` map, and the key is `r` and that count. A long rest ends the day with the calendar on too: dnd5e moves the clock for a rest only on a GM's client, so the GM keeps each character's own day in the actor-state setting's `restedInto` map: a long rest moves it to the day after the clock's day when the rest began, and the key is the later of that and the clock's day. A long rest ends a day, and a GM who then moves the clock over the same night doesn't make it two: the clock catches up. The borrow ahead of the clock is one day at most: a second long rest on the same calendar day (the GM's rest that left the clock on that day, then the player's own) ends no further day. A long rest taken while no GM is online is kept in that player's browser (localStorage, never the world: only a GM client writes) and sent once a GM is online, at that client's load or when the GM's loads and asks; the GM answers once it is counted and only then is it forgotten. The count is the unrefunded rows carrying that day's key, so the GM resets nothing and a refund gives a throw back. The shop names the odds ("Nothing 90%, Coin back 8%, Item 2%"), counts the day's throws and at the cap reads "The well is still until tomorrow (3 of 3 today)". The reveal is a coin arcing into dark water and its ripples, then a plink, the coin rising again, or a glow in the rarity's colour lifting the find; the result goes to the window's live region ("The coin sinks. Nothing."). The GM makes one with the manager's New well.

**Roulette, the Wheel of Fate** (`roulette.js`) is the first table game: the whole table bets at once and one spin settles every bet. The GM's wheel has N pockets (twelve by default: five common, four uncommon, two rare, one legendary), each numbered by its place and in one of two colours the GM names ("Evocation red" and "Abjuration black") and one rarity; the rim of each pocket is its rarity's colour, so the wheel is the odds ribbon bent into a circle. A bet is on a colour, odd or even, one number or a rarity band, each with exact odds from the pocket list (a colour's share, 1 in N for a number, the band's pockets) and the GM's payout multiplier per bet type; optionally a winning bet on a rarity band also draws an item of that rarity from the game's pools, granted at the spin and taken back by the ordinary Refund. The GM sets the least and most a bet can be, how many bets a character gets a round and the betting window (seconds, or until the GM presses Spin). The pocket is rolled with Foundry's own 1dN on the GM (`allowInteractive: false`, inside the write queue) at the spin, before anything is shown. The shop card says the odds per bet type, the bounds and what the round is doing ("Bets close in 0:42", "No round open yet"); "Place a bet" opens the table, and the GM's "Open a round" opens the round. One chat card per round, after the landing, names the pocket and every character's net.

**The table window** (`tablewindow.js`, with its words in `tableview.js`) is one room any table kind reuses: it opens on every client when the GM opens a round, shows every character's chips on the rail of bet spots as the bets arrive (the GM's "staked" broadcast, then the log), the clock or "waiting for the GM to spin", a player's bet form (the character, what to bet on, the pick, the stake, one "Place a bet" button that reads "Placing…" while the bet is out, a refused bet said on the line under it) and the GM's Spin. The reveal plays through the round's one hold: the disc turns and the ball runs the other way and slows into the pocket (one rotation transform each, one animation frame loop), ticks slowing with the ball, one soft drop as it dips, then the winning spots light, the rest step back, the vault washes in the pocket's rarity and the verdict ("The ball lands on 7, red, Uncommon.") and each character's net go to the window's live region. Reduced motion and a hidden page land at once. Screen readers hear the table in words: a visually hidden list of the bets, a status line for each new bet and the round's state.

**The Auction** (`auction.js`, VIB-192) is the second table game, on the same round and the same table window. The GM lists one lot from **New auction**: an item (any item in the pools, or one dropped on the editor from the Items sidebar or a compendium) or a sealed crate (one of the GM's crates, opened for the winner at the hammer with the crate's own roll and strip, as a fresh pull that counts toward no pity and takes none of its stock), with the opening bid, the least raise, a reserve (players see only whether it is met, never the amount), the gavel time (20 s by default) and optional house bidders (how many, each one's budget, and the chance each has to bid every 2 s). A bid is a stake: it holds its amount from the character's purse as an open Pull log row, and a higher bid by anyone refunds the one it beats in the same queued task, so a lot never holds more than one open bid and a losing bid is never charged. A bid must be at least the top bid plus the raise and within the purse, and a character can't outbid themselves; two bids at one amount sent together leave the second refused naming the new least bid. Every bid sets the gavel back to its full time; when it runs out, the GM's client brings it down itself (the round's one resolve): the top bid wins the lot and pays exactly the bid, the item going onto the sheet (or the crate's strip running for the whole table). A house win, a reserve not met, or a lot that is gone refunds the top bid. House bidders are rolled on the GM in the write queue, bid only against a player and never past their budget, and are log rows with no character and no coins. The table window shows the board: the lot, the top bid and its bidder, the reserve met or not, the last five bids (the beaten ones struck through), the gavel as a bar that drains and fills again on each bid, and the player's one primary action naming the next legal bid ("Bid 55 gp", with a field to bid more). Each bid is said in the window's status line ("House bidder 1 bids 40 gp, the top bid; Orla Vane's bid came back."), the verdict at the hammer goes to its live region, and the gavel's countdown is a timer, never read aloud each second. The GM can **Bring the gavel down** early or **Call it off** (every bid comes back). One chat card after the hammer. A GM who reloads mid-auction has the gavel brought down on load; an open bid whose gavel ran out more than the grace (10 minutes) before the load is refunded then. The GM's Refund on the winning row in the Pull log gives the bid back and takes the item off the sheet. Reduced motion lands the hammer at once.

**Table rounds** (`round.js`, `table.js`) are the core for games the whole table stakes on at once (Roulette and the Auction). A kind with `table: true` is staked, never pulled: the GM opens a round (a betting window with a clock, or open until the GM spins), each stake is charged on the GM in the write queue and written as a Pull log row with `status: "open"` in the same task, and one queued resolve rolls the outcome once, marks the round resolved, settles or refunds every open row in one log write and pays each character once through `settle()`. A second resolve does nothing. The guards run again at the resolve: a character that changed hands gets its stake back, not winnings, and a deleted one's row is closed. An open stake refunds with the ordinary Refund. When the GM's Foundry loads, any open stake whose round is gone, or stale (10 minutes after it closed, or after it opened with no clock), is refunded and the GM is told how many; while the GM stays, a timer at the next round to go stale (`round.js nextStaleAt`, `table.js watchStale`, re-armed on every change of the rounds) does the same, so a round left unspun comes back without a reload. The resolve is one broadcast carrying every stake's outcome and one hold for the whole table (the round's chat card stays sealed until this client lands; reduced motion and hidden pages land at once); a table kind's window plugs in with `setTableWindow`. Rows from before rounds have no status and read as settled. The Pull log folds a round's stakes under one line.

## Rarity overrides

A GM can change an item's rarity on its row in the pool editor. That rarity decides the drop odds, and it is the rarity the reveal, the chat card, the pull log and the character sheet all show: the granted copy carries it (one value, written to dnd5e 6's `system.rarities`). The source item in its compendium is never changed. The copy keeps where it came from: `flags["loot-crates"].sourceRarity` (the source's own rarities, empty when it had none) with `rarityOverride: true`, and `_stats.compendiumSource` for a compendium item; the log row reads "Legendary, set by GM (source Uncommon)". An entry left at its source's rarity is granted exactly as the source is, a multi-rarity ("Varies") item included. Price and attunement are not touched.

The shop shows each character their own odds. A box's pity rule and its no-duplicates rule apply to the character picked in the shop, the same way the roll applies them: items they already own are left out, and once pity is due only the guaranteed rarities show. The card says where they stand ("Rare or better within 4 pulls", or "guaranteed this pull"), and a box with nothing new for that character can't be bought ("Nothing new for <name>"). The reveal's odds bar shows the odds the shop showed.

## Making a game

Every **New** on the manager's Boxes tab (New box, New well, New dice game, New deck, New wheel, New auction) opens that kind's editor on an unsaved draft with the kind's defaults: nothing is written, and nothing reaches a player's shelf, until the dialog's **Add to shelf** (reading **Add** while the shop is closed). Cancel, Escape or closing the dialog leaves the shelf as it was, so there is never an empty "New box" row to delete, and a half-built dice game never takes stakes while the GM is still naming it. The dialog opens with the name focused. Once added, the manager shows the Boxes tab with the new row marked and its Edit focused and in view, and the tab's status line says where it went: "Gilded Crate is on the shelf at 250 gp." or "Gilded Crate added. The shop is closed, so players can't see it yet." A pool's bar on the Pools tab has **New crate from this pool**: the same editor on a crate named for the pool that draws from it alone, with the price focused, so a first crate is one dialog away from a pool with items in it. Every editor has an **On the shelf** box, ticked for a New: untick it to build a game ahead of the session and keep it from the players (the button then reads **Add**). Each row of the Boxes tab has an **On the shelf** toggle over its Edit, Copy and Delete, and the toolbar counts the games players can see ("7 of 8 on the shelf"): one click on a game off the shelf (its row dimmed and tagged "Off the shelf", its toggle unpressed: the toggle keeps the one label and its switch shows on or off) puts it in every open shop at once, and one click takes it back. A player's shop never lists a game off the shelf (the GM's shows it with its Buy off), and the GM refuses a pull or a stake on one ("<name> is no longer on the shelf. Nothing was charged."), so a shop drawn a moment before the change charges nothing. A wheel off the shelf opens no new round; one already open runs to its resolve. A record saved before this has no flag and is on the shelf (`rules.shelved: false` is off; nothing to migrate). **Copy** on a game's row opens the same editor on an unsaved copy of that game (`newgame.js` copyOf): every setting comes along (the pools and their shares, the weights, pity, no duplicates, the limits, the dice, the deck's cards, the wheel's pockets and payouts), none of its history does (the stock is full again, the deck's shuffle mark is fresh, and the log, each character's pity and collection and any live round stay the original's), and it is named "<name> (copy)" (a copy of that is "<name> (copy 2)", then 3 and on, never a name already on the Boxes tab) with the name focused for the GM to rename and reprice before **Add to shelf**. Cancel writes nothing.

## Starter crates

A new world's empty Pools and Boxes tabs offer **Add starter crates** (it stays on the Boxes toolbar after; `game.modules.get("loot-crates").api.addStarterCrates()` presses it too). One press fills four pools from the installed dnd5e system's own item compendiums, read by type, subtype and rarity from the packs the system lists (`dnd5e.items`, and `dnd5e.equipment24` where it exists; each pool from the pack of the rules the world plays by, the other only for a pool that pack has nothing for, so a 2024 table doesn't get both editions' potions under two names): Potions and scrolls (common and uncommon), Wondrous trinkets (uncommon and rare), Arms and armour (+1 and +2 weapons and armour) and Dragon's hoard (rare and up). The pools hold UUID references only, as every pool does. Then it adds one game of every kind on them: the Adventurer's cache (50 gp), the Arcane coffer (250 gp, pity after 5) and the Dragon's hoard chest (1,000 gp, pity after 10, 3 in stock), a Wishing Well, Dragon's Teeth with a perfect-roll prize, a Deck of Many Things with the five placeholder cards a New deck has, a roulette wheel on the default pockets, and an auction of the sealed hoard chest. Every game is added **off the shelf**: players see nothing until the GM puts each one out from its row, and the status line says so. They are ordinary records, edited, copied and deleted like any other. A pack the world can't open is named in the status line and the rest are used. Pressed again, it says what of the set is already here and offers to add the games again as copies drawing from the starter pools already there; Cancel writes nothing.

## The welcome and what's new

The first time a GM loads a world with the module on, a welcome window says what it does, where the shop and the manager are (Crates and Manage crates in the Items sidebar) and three first steps, with **Add starter crates** (the primary in a world with no games yet), **Open the manager** (the primary otherwise), **Read the guide** (the README) and **Don't show again**. Focus starts on the primary; Escape closes it. After an update, a GM gets "What's new in X" once instead: the lines of `module/scripts/changelog.js`, which mirror the newest entry of `CHANGELOG.md`. Closing either window keeps it closed (the world setting `loot-crates.welcome` records the version seen); **Don't show again** turns off both for good, and **Game Settings, Configure Settings, Loot Crates, Show the welcome** opens the welcome again (closing it turns the update notes back on). The same version again or a downgrade shows nothing; players never see either. `game.modules.get("loot-crates").api.openWelcome()` opens it too.

## The module's words

Every sentence a player or GM reads, from a shop card to a PullError to a Game Settings hint, is a key in `module/lang/en.json` under one namespace (`LOOTCRATES.<Area>.<Name>`, such as `LOOTCRATES.Shop.Title`), declared in `module.json`'s `languages`. The scripts read them through one pure module, `i18n.js`: `t(key, data)` fills `{name}` placeholders from `data` as Foundry's `game.i18n.format` does, and a sentence with a number or a name in it is one key with placeholders, never pieces joined in code. `t()` asks Foundry first: `main.js` installs `game.i18n` at `i18nInit` for every key Foundry has, so a translation in `lang/<code>.json` wins. A key Foundry doesn't know comes from the module's own `lang/en.json`, which `main.js` fetches as the script loads (`loadStrings`, resolved beside `scripts/`), so a copy whose language file Foundry never read (the working copy a live check imports beside an installed release) still reads in English; the harness loads the same file before its first render, and the tests from `test/setup-i18n.js`. A key in neither comes back as itself. Nothing reads a word when a script loads: the words arrive after the scripts, so a table of labels is `words()` (getters), a text constant is an `export let` kept current by `onStrings()`, a window's title is its own getter, and a Game Settings entry's name and hint are getters Foundry reads when it renders. What the module writes into the world (a new pool's name, a log row's "Nothing") is written in the words of the GM who wrote it; the starter set's pool and game names, dnd5e's rarity keys and coin abbreviations, compendium labels and item names are data, not words. `test/i18n.test.js` holds the file to the scripts: every key a script names is in it, every key in it is named, each call's data fills its sentence's placeholders, and no value is empty or an emoji.

## Making a crate from a roll table

A GM can turn a roll table into a crate in one step: **Make a crate** on the table's menu in the Roll Tables sidebar (right-click it), or drop the table on the manager's Boxes tab. The table's items go into a new pool named for it (store.js entriesFromDrop), and the crate editor opens on an unsaved draft named for the table, drawing from that pool, with the price focused: type a price and press **Add to shelf**. Cancel takes the new pool away again unless something else has come to use it. A table with no item results says so and makes nothing.

- **The table's odds.** The crate draws by entry weight alone (`rules.oddsMode: "weight"`), not by rarity band: a pool by its share, then an item by its weight, so a result that is 1 in 100 on the table stays 1 in 100. The editor shows the choice as **Odds: By rarity** or **Use the table's odds**, each with its sentence; on the table's odds the rarity weights are hidden (kept for a switch back). Pity and no-duplicates still work: what the character owns is left out, and a due pity keeps only the items at its rarity or better, by their weights. The strip's fillers follow the same weights and the near-miss tease is the same honest step up. A record with no `oddsMode` draws by rarity, exactly as before.
- **The shop names the real chances.** The card lists the best finds first by name with their chances ("Flame Tongue <1%"), up to four, then "and 3 more 56%", then "Nothing" when the crate can miss; the chances add up to 100 and never read 0%. The editor's preview names up to eight; the Boxes row says "the table's odds".
- **Nested tables are flattened.** A result that rolls another table brings in that table's items with the weights multiplied through (the result's weight times each item's share of its table), down to five tables deep; a table nested deeper, or one rolling a table above it, is skipped and counted. Weights stay whole numbers, scaled together so every ratio is exact.
- **Text results** ("You find nothing") are left out by default. The editor's **Count text results as nothing** (off for a new crate) makes their share of the table the share of pulls that give no item (`rules.missChance`): the core's miss, rolled before the item and never on a pull whose pity is due. The strip lands on a blank, the reveal says "Nothing this time." with the buyer's gold, the chat card says Nothing (sealed like any crate's until the spin lands), the pull counts toward pity, and the Pull log row refunds like any other.
- **Copied, not linked.** The editor says "Copied from <table> (N items, M nested tables flattened); edit the table and make the crate again to pick up changes." Editing the table later changes nothing until Make a crate is run again.

Dropping a roll table on a pool still adds its items to that pool after the preview, which now says which nested tables it flattened and how many text results it skipped.

## Stock and limits

Every editor field has a name of its own for a screen reader: each pool's checkbox is named for the pool and its share "Share of pulls from <pool>", off while the pool is unticked (an unticked pool is never drawn from, so its share does nothing); the pity count is "Pulls before the guarantee" ("Throws ..." at a well), the wheel's bounds "Smallest bet in gp" and "Largest bet in gp, blank for any", and a deck's card fields are numbered by the card's place ("Weight of card 2"). The shared rows live in `editorfields.js`, so a new field gets a name the same way. The Boxes and Pull log tables' button column is named "Actions" for a screen reader. Each box's editor has one **Limits** group. **Stock** is how many there are in all (blank for unlimited): the editor and the shop say what is left of it ("3 of 10 left"), each sale takes one, a refund puts one back (never past the GM's number), and **Restock** fills it again when you press Save (the line beside it says "10 of 10 left once you save"; Cancel leaves the stock as it was). Changing the number moves what is left by the same amount. **Each character may buy N in all, or N a day**: counted for the buyer's character from its unrefunded Pull log rows at that game (a day is the world's calendar, or a character's long rest with dnd5e's calendar off, `clock.js`, as for the well's cap), so a refund gives the purchase back and nothing is reset by hand. The GM checks it inside the write queue (`pull.js`), so two quick buys can't both pass, for every game that is pulled (the crate, the dice and the deck offer it in their editors); the shop card says "You can buy 1 more" and, once reached, "Limit reached: 2 of 2 for Crate Tester" with its Buy off. The record keeps `rules.stock` (left), `rules.stockMax` (the GM's number) and `rules.perCharacter` (`{ max, per: "ever" | "day" }`); a record from before them reads "3 left" and has no limit, as before. Every pull's log row now carries the in-game day it was bought on (`day`).

A refund from the Pull log undoes the whole pull: the coins come back, the granted item leaves the sheet, the box's stock goes up by one, and the character's pity count and no-duplicates record for that box go back to what they would be without that pull (the confirm dialog says where the count goes and that the item can drop again). Each log row keeps the pity count it was pulled at, whether the character already had the item, and whether the pull reset the count (`before.sinceRare`, `before.hadItem`, `resetPity`), so a refund frees the item even when an older row granted it before the record was reset by hand; a row written by 0.6.0 or earlier has none of these, so its refund corrects the no-duplicates record and the pull count and leaves the pity count as it is.

## The result waits for the spin

Everything that would tell a client the result waits until that client's spin lands (or is skipped or closed). The chat card shows "Opening…" in place of the item; a game whose card only some outcomes post (the dice's win, the well's find) has the whole message hidden until then, since the card turning up at all would tell. On a character sheet, the granted item's row stays hidden: anything that names it by `data-item-id`, in sheets already open and sheets opened mid-spin. In the shop, that crate's card keeps the character's pity line and odds from before the pull, then changes when the spin lands. Display only: nothing is written, and the item is on the sheet from the moment it is granted. A tab that is hidden, or set to reduced motion, lands at once, so nothing is held there. A replay holds nothing.

## Watch again

A pull's chat card carries a small display-only snapshot of its spin (`flags["loot-crates"].replay`: each card's name, art and rarity, where it lands, the odds and the result; no uuids, no purse, no granted item, about 6 KB). Anyone at the table can press **Watch again** on the card to replay that spin on their own screen, in a window titled "Replay: ...". A replay writes nothing, posts nothing, needs no GM and shows nobody's gold. Refunded cards and cards posted before this feature have no button.

A reveal that this client didn't see spin (it landed while the tab was hidden, the tab was hidden mid-spin, or Skip was pressed) also offers **Watch again** beside Close. It replays the same strip in the same window, display only, reading Skip while it plays; once a replay has run to its end the button goes. It never appears after a spin that played out, or with reduced motion on.

```
npm run harness       # serve the harness at http://localhost:5281/harness/ (?theme=light for the light theme)
```

The harness runs the module's pure scripts and its stylesheet with sample data outside Foundry so the shop, the spin and the pool editor can be looked at without a world. The stylesheet borrows dnd5e's fonts and textures and Foundry's item icons; set `FOUNDRY_URL=http://<foundry>:30000` for `npm run harness` or `npm run smoke` and the harness passes those requests through to that world, so it renders with the real assets (without it, system fonts and plain grounds stand in). `foundry-dev/` has the headless runner for a real check against a dev world.

## Layout

- `module/` is what ships: `module.json`, `scripts/`, `styles/`, `lang/en.json` (every word, by key).
  - Pure, tested, no Foundry globals: `i18n.js` (the module's words: `t()`, the localizer Foundry's `game.i18n` is installed as, the English table and `loadStrings()`), `kinds.js` (the games of chance and their contract), `crate.js` (the crate: the first kind's roll, spin, card), `well.js` (the Wishing Well: its roll, cap, reveal, card and folded log), `dicegame.js` (Dragon's Teeth: the verdict, the exact odds, Foundry's Roll read back as faces, the tumbling dice and the 3D-dice wait, its card), `deck.js` (the Deck of Many Things: the cards as written, the plan that leaves drawn once cards out, the rising price from the log, the roll for every kind of card, the one card's lift, hold and turn, its words, card and editor), `round.js` (the table round's state: open stakes, the stale window, the resolve's plan, the log's round lines), `roulette.js` (the Wheel of Fate: the pockets, the exact odds per bet type, the bets, the spin with Foundry's die, the settle, the wheel and the rail, its words, card and editor), `auction.js` (the Auction: the lot, the bid checks, the house bidders, the hammer and its settle, the board with the gavel's bar and the sealed crate's strip, its words, card and editor), `tableview.js` (the shared table window's words and markup), `clock.js` (the in-game day's key), `limits.js` (a game's stock and its limit per character: the words, the count from the log and the editor's Limits group), `shelf.js` (whether players can see a game: the shop's filter, the GM's refusal, the editor's box and the Boxes row's toggle), `newgame.js` (what each New on the Boxes tab starts from: the draft per kind, the dialog's words, the status line once it is on the shelf), `starter.js` (the starter crates: the dnd5e packs read, the four pools picked by type, subtype and rarity, one game of every kind, the empty tabs' offer and the status line), `welcome.js` (the GM's welcome and the note after an update: when each shows, what a close records, the words and markup), `changelog.js` (the newest version's lines for that note, kept in step with CHANGELOG.md), `boxestable.js` (the Boxes tab's toolbar and table, every kind tagged and every cell captioned so a narrow pane stacks the rows, each price a field typed in place, shared with the harness), `route.js` (the ways between the sidebar, the shop and the manager: the sidebar buttons, the Roll Tables menu entry in both Foundry versions' shape, the manager's nav with View shop, the shop's Manage crates and its empty shelf, shared with the harness), `logtable.js` (the Pull log's table: misses folded, rounds grouped, the notes under a row, shared with the harness), `odds.js` (the pull and the shop's odds), `currency.js` (coins, `settle()` for every game's charge and payout, and `parsePrice()`, the inverse of how a price is shown), `boxcard.js` (the shop's box card and odds pills, shared with the manager and the harness), `revealcard.js` (the reveal's result block, where its window opens, and the replay snapshot a chat card keeps, shared with the harness), `chatcard.js` (what a pull's chat card becomes after a refund, and finding that card), `poolview.js` (the pool editor's rows, search, filter chips and 50-row pages, shared with the harness), `strip.js` (the reveal row), `spin.js` (the animation, DOM only), `redraw.js` (which open windows redraw when a world setting changes, once per burst of writes).
  - Foundry-side: `store.js` (settings-backed pools/boxes/log), `starterbuild.js` (reads the dnd5e indexes and writes the starter crates in one queued task), `welcomewindow.js` (the welcome window, its world setting, the Show the welcome menu and the check at load), `pull.js` (one purchase of any kind: the money path), `socket.js` (player to GM), `table.js` (a table round on the GM: open, close, resolve once, the load's refund of stale stakes), `tablewindow.js` (the shared table window: the chips as bets arrive, the clock, the bet form, the GM's Spin, the round's reveal through the one hold), `shop.js`, `reveal.js`, `dicereveal.js` (the Dice So Nice bridge), `manager.js`, `chat.js`, `sound.js`, `main.js`.
- `harness/` is the no-Foundry page used by `npm run smoke`.
- `test/` is Vitest. `scripts/` are the checks.
