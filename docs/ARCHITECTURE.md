# Loot Crates: how it works

The developer's map of the module: how each game runs on the one core, what the data records hold, and where each script sits. The player and GM guide is the [README](../README.md); the design tokens and rules are in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Overview

A Foundry VTT module for dnd5e. The GM builds **pools** of items (dragged in from any compendium, the Items sidebar or another module's imports, or bulk-added by filter), prices **boxes** that draw from those pools at chosen rarity odds, and players buy a box with their character's coins. The pull plays as a crate-opening spin that everyone at the table watches, and the item lands in the character's inventory.

- Players: the **Crates** button at the top of the Items sidebar opens the shop.
- GM: **Manage crates** beside it opens the manager on its Boxes tab: boxes, pools, and the pull log with refunds (so does **Game Settings, Loot Crates**). The manager's **View shop** opens the shop, and the shop's header controls hold **Manage crates** for the way back.
- Prices are typed the way the shop shows them, "50 gp", "5 sp" or "12 gp 5 sp" (a bare number is gold), in each Boxes row's price field (saved on Enter or Tab) and in the editors.
- Purchases go over the module socket to the active GM's client, which rolls, deducts, grants and broadcasts the reveal. No GM online means the shop is closed.

## Games of chance

Every game the module runs, the crate first, the Wishing Well, the dice, the deck and the scratch tickets, is a record in the `boxes` setting with a `kind` (a record with none is a crate, so nothing written before kinds existed changes). The core is one copy for all of them: the money path on the GM (`pull.js`: the guards, the roll, the charge and any payout through one `settle()`, the grant with its rollback), the request, queue and broadcast (`socket.js`), the Pull log row and the refund (`refund.js`: coins back, payout taken back, item removed, stock and state restored, card marked), and the hold until the spin lands (`reveal.js`: the sealed chat card, the hidden sheet row, the held shop odds). A kind (`kinds.js` has the contract) supplies what differs: the roll, the reveal's animation and result block, the chat card and the shop card. A roll may end in no item (a miss) or in coins paid out: log rows carry `payoutCp` (rows from before it count as 0), and a refund that would have to take back winnings the character has spent is refused, naming the shortfall. Another module adds a game with `game.modules.get("loot-crates").api.registerKind`.

**Dragon's Teeth** (`dicegame.js`) is the tavern dice game, one variant for now: "beat the dragon". The GM sets the stake, the dice (2d6 or 3d6), the dragon's number and the payout multiplier, and can let a perfect roll (every die on its top face) win an item from the game's pools too. A player bets the stake; the GM's client rolls Foundry's own dice (`new Roll(formula).evaluate({ allowInteractive: false })`, inside the write queue, so a GM on manual dice entry is never prompted mid-purchase); a total over the dragon's number wins the stake times the multiplier through the same `settle()`, a total equal to it loses. The faces are decided and logged before any client shows a die, and travel in the reveal as the serialised Roll, so every client shows the same dice; no ChatMessage ever carries the Roll (that would print the total everywhere the moment it was created). The reveal is the dragon's number over three dice that tumble and settle left to right, then the total and the verdict in the live region ("15 beats the dragon's 12 · Rolled 4, 6, 5 on 3d6 · Won 30 gp · 1020 gp in the purse"); with Dice So Nice active the window waits for its 3D roll instead (a ~6 s fallback covers a stalled animation), then shows the faces and the total and lands a frame later, so the total is told before the landing on both paths. A win posts a card with the faces and the verdict, sealed like a crate's until this client's dice land; a loss is quiet, with a word for the buyer alone. The shop card says the rule and the exact odds ("Win 26%, Lose 74%" for 3d6 over 12), what a win pays and whether a perfect roll wins an item, and refuses a dragon the dice can't beat. A refund takes the winnings back less the stake in one move; a refund the purse can't cover (the winnings spent) is refused with the shortfall named, never minted.

The **Deck of Many Things** (`deck.js`) is one card drawn from a deck the GM writes in the manager (New deck): each card is a name, what it does (an item from the deck's pools at rarity odds, coins paid out, coins taken, an effect whose text the GM plays out, or a blank), a weight and whether it is drawn once per deck. Nothing copyrighted ships; the five default cards are placeholders. A once card drawn leaves the deck until the GM presses Reshuffle, read from the pull log the way the well's cap is, so a refund puts the card back by itself. The price rises with each draw in a row (the first draw at the record's price, then base × step, base × step², as long as each draw follows the last within the deck's window: two minutes unless the GM sets another in the deck editor, "Draws count as in a row within N minutes", where blank or 0 makes every draw a first draw; the window is stored on the deck, and a deck saved before it reads two minutes), charged by the core (`pull.js` takes the kind's price of a play) and written to the row, so a refund gives back what was paid and takes the step back. A loss card takes its gold through the same one `settle()` as a negative payout, never more than the purse holds after the stake. The reveal is one card that lifts, holds face-down under three soft ticks, then turns; every draw posts a sealed chat card (an effect's text in it, for the GM); the buyer alone gets **Draw again for N gp** beside Close, which sends the next draw as an ordinary pull and closes the window once the next has opened. A loss is the one thing in the module that means "you lost something": a cold slate token (`--lc-bane`), a dimmed vault and two falling notes.

**Scratch tickets** (`scratch.js`, VIB-188) are a printed roll: the GM prints N tickets with a fixed prize structure (so many of each prize line, the rest duds), and each Buy draws one ticket, one line, from what is left of the roll, without replacement. A line is what one ticket wins, as real games print them: one part (coins, an item at a rarity from the ticket's pools, or the bonus spot's gem) or several at once ("Flame Tongue + 25 gp + the gem", two items), stored as `{ count, parts: [{ gp } | { item: { rarity } } | { bonus: true }] }`. A row from before lines (`{ count, gp }`, `{ count, item }`, `{ count, bonus }`) reads as a line of its one part (`rowParts`) and is written back as a line only when the GM saves the editor; a line's key names its parts best first (`gp.25`, `item.rare+gp.25+bonus`), so a one-part line keeps the key its tier always had and old log rows still count. Because the draw is one line per ticket, a ticket that pays twice is exactly as rare as printed, and the odds readout counts lines; its guarantee ("all prizes 5 gp or more") compares each line's total. Nothing is stored for the roll beyond its definition and its id (`roll: { id, size, prizes }`): the tickets sold are the unrefunded Pull log rows at that game for that roll id, each naming its prize line, the way the well's cap and the deck's once cards are counted, so a refund puts the ticket and its prize back by itself, and two Buys in the write queue never draw the same last ticket. Sold out is a real state (the GM refuses the Buy before anything is charged); **Print a new roll** on the Boxes row moves the record to the next roll id with the same prize list, and the old roll's rows stay counted against the old roll. A line whose items the pools can't give stays in the roll, undrawn, until they can. Two layouts: **lucky numbers** (a row of lucky numbers and a grid of the player's numbers, each with a prize under it; every match wins its prize and wins add up, a coin prize sometimes split across two panels; a bonus spot pays on top on a bonus ticket) and **match 3** (every panel hides a symbol; three the same win that symbol's prize). The card is laid out on the GM from the decided ticket with the roll's rng (`layCard`) and rides the reveal broadcast, so every client draws the same card; it obeys the lotteries' honesty rules (your numbers never two the same, a match only where a win was decided, the bonus mark only on a bonus ticket; on a match 3 card three of a symbol only where a win was decided and every other symbol at most twice), and `cardWins` reads its wins back from the layout alone, which the tests hold to the decided line over thousands of seeded tickets. Every part of a line is laid out: each coin and item part on a winning panel of its own (under a lucky number of its own, shared only when a line has more wins than the card has lucky numbers; on match 3, a three of a kind of its own symbol, the key holding every amount and rarity the roll's lines pay, at most eight), the gem in the bonus spot. A line the card can't show (on lucky numbers more paying parts than the card has panels; on match 3 two parts the same, more sets of three than the grid holds, past the key's eight) is left out of the roll, and the editor names it and keeps Save off (`scratchPreview` `refused`). Each item of a line is drawn the crate's way at its rarity and granted through the core's `grants` (one creation, one row with `itemIds`, one refund that removes them all); the coins and the gem are one `payoutCp` through the core's `settle()`. The reveal names every item and the coins, the chat card links each item, and the replay snapshot keeps the line's items (display only). **Players see what is left in the roll** (`showLeft`, on unless the GM turns it off): off, a player's shop card shows only the price, the roll's top prize and its guarantee, as a printed ticket does, and the ticket marks itself `hidden` so the reveal and the ticket's foot say no counts; the GM's manager and the GM's own shop show everything, the shop card saying the counts are hidden. Hidden means hidden from the player's browser too: the reveal broadcast of a hidden game carries no `left`, no `stateBefore.scratch` and a ticket of `{ rollId, hidden: true }` alone (`revealExtra`; the GM's log row keeps the ticket's place and prize line), so a shop has nothing to hold for it and holds nothing, and the chat card's replay snapshot keeps no counts either (`cleanTicket`). The one place left is the Pull log: a world setting, which Foundry sends to every client, naming each sold ticket's prize line. **Fill the prize list** (`scratchplan.js`, pure) builds a prize list from a few numbers, the way real games are printed: `generatePrizeLines({ price, size, payback, winEvery, top, combos, bonus, items, layout, grid, rng })` gives `round(size / winEvery)` winning tickets whose coins (and the gem) come within a few percent of payback x size x price; the lowest prize is the price and the most common, the prizes climb at least half again a step (multiples of the price) with counts never rising, the top prize (an item at a rarity, coins, or an amount worked out from the roll) comes once or as often as asked, and by `combos` some middle prizes are combinations of smaller coin amounts, the gem or an item (valued at `config.js ITEM_VALUE`, the low end of the DMG's price band for its rarity) adding up to the prize. Items are not counted toward the payback, since one rare item outweighs a cheap roll's whole take; the readout says the payback in coins and the items apart (`paybackOf`). It tries up to 60 random structures with the rng, counting each with `solveCounts` (a falling spread, then single tickets moved between prizes while the coins come nearer) and nudging plain coin prizes a step when that helps, and keeps the first within 2% (structures with too few steps or a cliff between two counts scored as further off); every line must pass `lineMisfit` and survive `prizesOf` as written (on match 3, the key's eight symbols). The editor's Fill fields (`fillFieldsHTML`, `wireFill`, plain DOM like `wireScratchEditor`) write the lines into the rows, asking first (the manager's `DialogV2.confirm`) when the rows hold anything Fill didn't write itself; Shuffle draws again and never returns the list it replaces; nothing is saved until Save, and the fields aren't stored on the record. The shop card shows the price, the tickets left, each prize left of its count (a prize gone struck through), the odds of what is left ("1 in 2 tickets wins, the top prize (Rare item) 1 in 57, all prizes 5 gp or more") and **Buy a ticket for 5 gp**; while a ticket is still being opened on a client, that client's shop holds the counts from before it (`reveal.js heldBefore`), whoever bought it, so no shop strikes a prize from the list before the card is open. The editor (New scratch ticket) sets the price, the layout and grid, the bonus spot, the roll's size and its prize list as rows, with a live readout of the roll as printed. Every ticket posts a sealed chat card with a small card marking the winning panels and what it paid; the buyer gets **Another ticket for 5 gp** beside Close while the roll has tickets. The reveal is the scratch (`mountScratch`, the foil in `views/foil.js`), as the approved mockup has it: the buyer scratches by hand, everyone else watches the same coin scrape the card by itself, and the window lands once every panel is open; the result never depends on the scratching, since the card arrived decided. Over the buyer's foil the pointer is hidden and a gold coin follows it, tilted into the stroke and pressed down while the button is held; the scrape is the coin's edge (a 32 px band with round caps and a ragged rim, up to 1.35x as wide at speed), erased from each panel's foil canvas inside the pointer's own events (a pointer-driven mask, not an animation). What moves by itself rides one requestAnimationFrame loop for the whole ticket and only while something moves: the shavings (one canvas over the ticket, at most 160 curls), the 60% check of a panel scratched since the last frame (a coarse grid of samples the band has passed over), the 160 ms crumble once a panel gives, and the table's sweep (380 ms a panel, the whole card in about five seconds at most). A panel says only "Panel 3, covered" until it opens, and a winning panel is lit only once open (on a match 3 card once the third of its symbol is). Keyboard: the panels are one tab stop; the arrows, Home and End move between them, Space or Enter opens one, R reveals the card (kept from Foundry's own keybindings), and **Reveal all** sits under the ticket; Skip and Close open everything too. Reduced motion: the table's card opens at once; the buyer still scratches, and a panel just opens at 60%. Sounds (`sound.js`): a rasp (looped deep brown noise through a bandpass whose pitch and level follow the coin's speed, 450 to 1350 Hz and .025 to .09, then a lowpass at 1.9 kHz) from pointerdown to pointerup, a soft 880 Hz tick per panel, then the sting: an item's rarity sting, a coin win's four rising notes, a dud's two low falling ones. The chat card keeps the card as its replay snapshot (`reveal.snapshot`, display only), so **Watch again** replays the scratch as the table saw it.

The **Wishing Well** (`well.js`) is the quiet game: a copper or silver thrown in, almost always nothing, now and then the coin back, rarely an item from its pools at rarity odds, with the crate's pity rule counting throws. Every throw writes a log row, so every throw is refundable; the Pull log folds a character's misses for a day into one line ("5 throws, nothing") with Refund one and a Hide misses switch; only an item posts a chat card. Each character may throw N times per in-game day: the day is the world's calendar (`game.time.components`, `clock.js`) while dnd5e's calendar is on. With it off (dnd5e 6's default, where a long rest doesn't move the clock) a day ends at each character's long rest, the way dnd5e recovers daily uses: `dnd5e.restCompleted` with `newDay` reaches the active GM (a player's client sends a `newDay` socket message, counted only for a sender who owns the character), which counts it in the actor-state setting's `days` map, and the key is `r` and that count. A long rest ends the day with the calendar on too: dnd5e moves the clock for a rest only on a GM's client, so the GM keeps each character's own day in the actor-state setting's `restedInto` map: a long rest moves it to the day after the clock's day when the rest began, and the key is the later of that and the clock's day. A long rest ends a day, and a GM who then moves the clock over the same night doesn't make it two: the clock catches up. The borrow ahead of the clock is one day at most: a second long rest on the same calendar day (the GM's rest that left the clock on that day, then the player's own) ends no further day. A long rest taken while no GM is online is kept in that player's browser (localStorage, never the world: only a GM client writes) and sent once a GM is online, at that client's load or when the GM's loads and asks; the GM answers once it is counted and only then is it forgotten. The count is the unrefunded rows carrying that day's key, so the GM resets nothing and a refund gives a throw back. The shop names the odds ("Nothing 90%, Coin back 8%, Item 2%"), counts the day's throws and at the cap reads "The well is still until tomorrow (3 of 3 today)". The reveal is a coin arcing into dark water and its ripples, then a plink, the coin rising again, or a glow in the rarity's colour lifting the find; the result goes to the window's live region ("The coin sinks. Nothing."). The GM makes one with the manager's New well.

**Roulette, the Wheel of Fate** (`roulette.js`) is the first table game: the whole table bets at once and one spin settles every bet. The GM's wheel has N pockets (twelve by default: five common, four uncommon, two rare, one legendary), each numbered by its place and in one of two colours the GM names ("Evocation red" and "Abjuration black") and one rarity; the rim of each pocket is its rarity's colour, so the wheel is the odds ribbon bent into a circle. A bet is on a colour, odd or even, one number or a rarity band, each with exact odds from the pocket list (a colour's share, 1 in N for a number, the band's pockets) and the GM's payout multiplier per bet type; optionally a winning bet on a rarity band also draws an item of that rarity from the game's pools, granted at the spin and taken back by the ordinary Refund. The GM sets the least and most a bet can be, how many bets a character gets a round and the betting window (seconds, or until the GM presses Spin). The pocket is rolled with Foundry's own 1dN on the GM (`allowInteractive: false`, inside the write queue) at the spin, before anything is shown. The shop card says the odds per bet type, the bounds and what the round is doing ("Bets close in 0:42", "No round open yet"); "Place a bet" opens the table, and the GM's "Open a round" opens the round. One chat card per round, after the landing, names the pocket and every character's net.

**The table window** (`tablewindow.js`, with its words in `tableview.js`) is one room any table kind reuses: it opens on every client when the GM opens a round, shows every character's chips on the rail of bet spots as the bets arrive (the GM's "staked" broadcast, then the log), the clock or "waiting for the GM to spin", a player's bet form (the character, what to bet on, the pick, the stake, one "Place a bet" button that reads "Placing…" while the bet is out, a refused bet said on the line under it) and the GM's Spin. The reveal plays through the round's one hold: the disc turns and the ball runs the other way and slows into the pocket (one rotation transform each, one animation frame loop), ticks slowing with the ball, one soft drop as it dips, then the winning spots light, the rest step back, the vault washes in the pocket's rarity and the verdict ("The ball lands on 7, red, Uncommon.") and each character's net go to the window's live region. Reduced motion and a hidden page land at once. Screen readers hear the table in words: a visually hidden list of the bets, a status line for each new bet and the round's state.

**The Auction** (`auction.js`, VIB-192) is the second table game, on the same round and the same table window. The GM lists one lot from **New auction**: an item (any item in the pools, or one dropped on the editor from the Items sidebar or a compendium) or a sealed crate (one of the GM's crates, opened for the winner at the hammer with the crate's own roll and strip, as a fresh pull that counts toward no pity and takes none of its stock), with the opening bid, the least raise, a reserve (players see only whether it is met, never the amount), the gavel time (20 s by default) and optional house bidders (how many, each one's budget, and the chance each has to bid every 2 s). A bid is a stake: it holds its amount from the character's purse as an open Pull log row, and a higher bid by anyone refunds the one it beats in the same queued task, so a lot never holds more than one open bid and a losing bid is never charged. A bid must be at least the top bid plus the raise and within the purse, and a character can't outbid themselves; two bids at one amount sent together leave the second refused naming the new least bid. Every bid sets the gavel back to its full time; when it runs out, the GM's client brings it down itself (the round's one resolve): the top bid wins the lot and pays exactly the bid, the item going onto the sheet (or the crate's strip running for the whole table). A house win, a reserve not met, or a lot that is gone refunds the top bid. House bidders are rolled on the GM in the write queue, bid only against a player and never past their budget, and are log rows with no character and no coins. The table window shows the board: the lot, the top bid and its bidder, the reserve met or not, the last five bids (the beaten ones struck through), the gavel as a bar that drains and fills again on each bid, and the player's one primary action naming the next legal bid ("Bid 55 gp", with a field to bid more). Each bid is said in the window's status line ("House bidder 1 bids 40 gp, the top bid; Orla Vane's bid came back."), the verdict at the hammer goes to its live region, and the gavel's countdown is a timer, never read aloud each second. The GM can **Bring the gavel down** early or **Call it off** (every bid comes back). One chat card after the hammer. A GM who reloads mid-auction has the gavel brought down on load; an open bid whose gavel ran out more than the grace (10 minutes) before the load is refunded then. The GM's Refund on the winning row in the Pull log gives the bid back and takes the item off the sheet. Reduced motion lands the hammer at once.

**Table rounds** (`round.js`, `table.js`) are the core for games the whole table stakes on at once (Roulette and the Auction). A kind with `table: true` is staked, never pulled: the GM opens a round (a betting window with a clock, or open until the GM spins), each stake is charged on the GM in the write queue and written as a Pull log row with `status: "open"` in the same task, and one queued resolve rolls the outcome once, marks the round resolved, settles or refunds every open row in one log write and pays each character once through `settle()`. A second resolve does nothing. The guards run again at the resolve: a character that changed hands gets its stake back, not winnings, and a deleted one's row is closed. An open stake refunds with the ordinary Refund. When the GM's Foundry loads, any open stake whose round is gone, or stale (10 minutes after it closed, or after it opened with no clock), is refunded and the GM is told how many; while the GM stays, a timer at the next round to go stale (`round.js nextStaleAt`, `table.js watchStale`, re-armed on every change of the rounds) does the same, so a round left unspun comes back without a reload. The resolve is one broadcast carrying every stake's outcome and one hold for the whole table (the round's chat card stays sealed until this client lands; reduced motion and hidden pages land at once); a table kind's window plugs in with `setTableWindow`. Rows from before rounds have no status and read as settled. The Pull log folds a round's stakes under one line.

## Tickets you carry

A ticket is one play of a pulled game (a crate, the Wishing Well, Dragon's Teeth, the deck, a scratch ticket; never a table game) as an item a character carries (`games/ticket.js`): an ordinary dnd5e consumable with the game's name, an image for its kind, the game's price as its value, quantity for a stack, one `utility` activity named for the play ("Open", "Scratch", "Throw", "Roll", "Draw") and the mark `flags["loot-crates"].ticket = { boxId, paid }` (`paid` what it cost, 0 for one handed out). **Make a ticket** on a pulled game's Boxes row creates one as a world Item in the Items folder "Loot Crates tickets" (found by its flag, made when missing); the GM drags it to a sheet, a loot sheet, a merchant or a pool (a crate's grant keeps the mark, so a ticket won is still a ticket).

The redeem path, one copy with a purchase's:

1. The player presses Use on the ticket on any sheet. dnd5e calls `dnd5e.preUseActivity` before its configuration dialog, consumption and chat card; `tickets.js ticketUseHook` sees the mark, returns `false` (dnd5e does nothing) and sends `{ type: "redeem", requestId, actorId, itemId, userId }` (`socket.js requestRedeem`), with the same timeout, wait notice and cancel as a pull. Any other item's use goes on.
2. The active GM runs it through `handlePull`, in the same write queue as a purchase: the user owns the character (`guardOwner`; the shop's switch and the shelf are the shop's, so a ticket plays with either off), then `tickets.js ticketToRedeem`: the item is still on the character, still a ticket, with quantity at least 1, and its game exists and is not a table game, else a `PullError` ("This ticket's game is gone"). What the row will keep of the ticket (`redeemRow`: its id, name, `paid` and its data as one ticket) is read now.
3. `pull.js performPull` with `ticket`: the purchase's guards but for the shelf and the stock (a ticket was sold already, so it neither checks nor takes stock, and a refund gives none back), the limit per character applies, the price is 0, and the kind's roll runs with its own guards (a sold-out scratch roll, the well's daily cap refuse here, before anything is spent). Then `playTicket`: one ticket spent (`spendTicket`: quantity down by one, the item deleted at the last), any payout settled, the items created; a failure at any step puts back what the steps before it wrote (the coins, then the ticket, made again with its id), so a ticket is spent exactly once and only when the play goes through.
4. The reveal broadcasts to everyone exactly as a purchase's, with `redeem: { itemName, paid }`, so the reveal's head and the chat card say "with a ticket" where a purchase says its price (`ticket.js priceTag`); the log row carries `price: 0` and `redeem` (the Pull log says "(ticket)", and a ticket's miss is never folded with paid ones).
5. A refund (`refund.js`) gives the ticket back (`tickets.js returnTicket`: one more on the same stack while it is still a ticket to that game, else made again from the row), takes back what the play won as any refund does, and returns no coins for the ticket itself; the refund card says the ticket came back.

**Buy to keep**, a purchase that plays nothing now: every pulled game's shop card has a second button under the Buy (`boxcard.js cardShell`, `data-action="keep"`, its game in `data-keep` so patchShop's morph never pairs it with the Buy), off for the same reasons and at the same price. `shop.js` sends `{ type: "keep", requestId, boxId, actorId, userId, price }` (`socket.js requestKeep`); the active GM runs `doKeep` in the write queue: the buyer's guards, then `pull.js performKeep` (`guardPlay` whole: shelf, stock, limit per character, the price on the button, the purse; one `settle()`; then `tickets.js keepTicket`, which puts a ticket marked `kept: true` with `paid` = the price into the pack, onto a kept stack of the same game at the same price when there is one; the coins come back if it can't), then the log row (the game's kind, box and price, `day`, `itemId: null` and `keep: { itemId, itemName, paid }`; nothing rolled, so no `rollId`, pity or state), one stock taken, the buyer answered with `kept` and a chat line posted (`chat.js postKeptCard`). A row that can't be written takes the ticket out and puts the coins back. No game counts a kept row as a play (the well's throws today, the deck's once cards, a scratch roll's tickets sold all skip `row.keep`); the deck's rising price counts it as the purchase it is (`deck.js pricedRows`: a kept ticket is charged at the streak's price and raises the next, and its later use doesn't raise it again), and so does the limit per character, and the later use of a kept ticket (its redeem row's `redeem.kept`) neither checks nor counts the limit again. A refund of a kept row (`refund.js`) takes one kept ticket of that game and price out of the pack (`games/ticket.js keptTicketFor`: the stack it went onto, else any other), returns the price and the stock, and leaves the buyer's state alone; with none left it is refused (`ticket-gone`) and nothing moves.

## Rarity overrides

A GM can change an item's rarity on its row in the pool editor. That rarity decides the drop odds, and it is the rarity the reveal, the chat card, the pull log and the character sheet all show: the granted copy carries it (one value, written to dnd5e 6's `system.rarities`). The source item in its compendium is never changed. The copy keeps where it came from: `flags["loot-crates"].sourceRarity` (the source's own rarities, empty when it had none) with `rarityOverride: true`, and `_stats.compendiumSource` for a compendium item; the log row reads "Legendary, set by GM (source Uncommon)". An entry left at its source's rarity is granted exactly as the source is, a multi-rarity ("Varies") item included. Price and attunement are not touched.

The shop shows each character their own odds. A box's pity rule and its no-duplicates rule apply to the character picked in the shop, the same way the roll applies them: items they already own are left out, and once pity is due only the guaranteed rarities show. The card says where they stand ("Rare or better within 4 pulls", or "guaranteed this pull"), and a box with nothing new for that character can't be bought ("Nothing new for <name>"). The reveal's odds bar shows the odds the shop showed.

## Making a game

Every **New** on the manager's Boxes tab (New box, New well, New dice game, New deck, New wheel, New auction, New scratch ticket) opens that kind's editor on an unsaved draft with the kind's defaults: nothing is written, and nothing reaches a player's shelf, until the dialog's **Add to shelf** (reading **Add** while the shop is closed). Cancel, Escape or closing the dialog leaves the shelf as it was, so there is never an empty "New box" row to delete, and a half-built dice game never takes stakes while the GM is still naming it. The dialog opens with the name focused. Once added, the manager shows the Boxes tab with the new row marked and its Edit focused and in view, and the tab's status line says where it went: "Gilded Crate is on the shelf at 250 gp." or "Gilded Crate added. The shop is closed, so players can't see it yet." A pool's bar on the Pools tab has **New crate from this pool**: the same editor on a crate named for the pool that draws from it alone, with the price focused, so a first crate is one dialog away from a pool with items in it. Every editor has an **On the shelf** box, ticked for a New: untick it to build a game ahead of the session and keep it from the players (the button then reads **Add**). Each row of the Boxes tab has an **On the shelf** toggle over its Edit, Copy and Delete, and the toolbar counts the games players can see ("7 of 8 on the shelf"): one click on a game off the shelf (its row dimmed and tagged "Off the shelf", its toggle unpressed: the toggle keeps the one label and its switch shows on or off) puts it in every open shop at once, and one click takes it back. A player's shop never lists a game off the shelf (the GM's shows it with its Buy off), and the GM refuses a pull or a stake on one ("<name> is no longer on the shelf. Nothing was charged."), so a shop drawn a moment before the change charges nothing. A wheel off the shelf opens no new round; one already open runs to its resolve. A record saved before this has no flag and is on the shelf (`rules.shelved: false` is off; nothing to migrate). **Copy** on a game's row opens the same editor on an unsaved copy of that game (`newgame.js` copyOf): every setting comes along (the pools and their shares, the weights, pity, no duplicates, the limits, the dice, the deck's cards, the wheel's pockets and payouts), none of its history does (the stock is full again, the deck's shuffle mark is fresh, and the log, each character's pity and collection and any live round stay the original's), and it is named "<name> (copy)" (a copy of that is "<name> (copy 2)", then 3 and on, never a name already on the Boxes tab) with the name focused for the GM to rename and reprice before **Add to shelf**. Cancel writes nothing.

## Starter crates

A new world's empty Pools and Boxes tabs offer **Add starter crates** (it stays on the Boxes toolbar after; `game.modules.get("loot-crates").api.addStarterCrates()` presses it too). One press fills four pools from the installed dnd5e system's own item compendiums, read by type, subtype and rarity from the packs the system lists (`dnd5e.items`, and `dnd5e.equipment24` where it exists; each pool from the pack of the rules the world plays by, the other only for a pool that pack has nothing for, so a 2024 table doesn't get both editions' potions under two names): Potions and scrolls (common and uncommon), Wondrous trinkets (uncommon and rare), Arms and armour (+1 and +2 weapons and armour) and Dragon's hoard (rare and up). The pools hold UUID references only, as every pool does. Then it adds one game of every kind on them: the Adventurer's cache (50 gp), the Arcane coffer (250 gp, pity after 5) and the Dragon's hoard chest (1,000 gp, pity after 10, 3 in stock), a Wishing Well, Dragon's Teeth with a perfect-roll prize, a Deck of Many Things with the five placeholder cards a New deck has, a roulette wheel on the default pockets, an auction of the sealed hoard chest, and two scratch tickets: the Market scratchie (1 gp, match 3, a roll of 50 with one uncommon potion or scroll) and the Fortune ticket (10 gp, lucky numbers with a 25 gp bonus spot, a roll of 100 with one rare trinket). Every game is added **off the shelf**: players see nothing until the GM puts each one out from its row, and the status line says so. They are ordinary records, edited, copied and deleted like any other. A pack the world can't open is named in the status line and the rest are used. Pressed again, it says what of the set is already here and offers to add the games again as copies drawing from the starter pools already there; Cancel writes nothing.

## The welcome and what's new

The first time a GM loads a world with the module on, a welcome window says what it does, where the shop and the manager are (Crates and Manage crates in the Items sidebar) and three first steps, with **Add starter crates** (the primary in a world with no games yet), **Open the manager** (the primary otherwise), **Read the guide** (the README) and **Don't show again**. Focus starts on the primary; Escape closes it. After an update, a GM gets "What's new in X" once instead: the lines of `module/scripts/core/changelog.js`, which mirror the newest entry of `CHANGELOG.md`. Closing either window keeps it closed (the world setting `loot-crates.welcome` records the version seen); **Don't show again** turns off both for good, and **Game Settings, Configure Settings, Loot Crates, Show the welcome** opens the welcome again (closing it turns the update notes back on). The same version again or a downgrade shows nothing; players never see either. `game.modules.get("loot-crates").api.openWelcome()` opens it too.

## The module's words

Every sentence a player or GM reads, from a shop card to a PullError to a Game Settings hint, is a key in `module/lang/en.json` under one namespace (`LOOTCRATES.<Area>.<Name>`, such as `LOOTCRATES.Shop.Title`), declared in `module.json`'s `languages`. The scripts read them through one pure module, `i18n.js`: `t(key, data)` fills `{name}` placeholders from `data` as Foundry's `game.i18n.format` does, and a sentence with a number or a name in it is one key with placeholders, never pieces joined in code. `t()` asks Foundry first: `main.js` installs `game.i18n` at `i18nInit` for every key Foundry has, so a translation in `lang/<code>.json` wins. A key Foundry doesn't know comes from the module's own `lang/en.json`, which `main.js` fetches as the script loads (`loadStrings`, resolved beside `scripts/`), so a copy whose language file Foundry never read (the working copy a live check imports beside an installed release) still reads in English; the harness loads the same file before its first render, and the tests from `test/setup-i18n.js`. A key in neither comes back as itself. Nothing reads a word when a script loads: the words arrive after the scripts, so a table of labels is `words()` (getters), a text constant is an `export let` kept current by `onStrings()`, a window's title is its own getter, and a Game Settings entry's name and hint are getters Foundry reads when it renders. What the module writes into the world (a new pool's name, a log row's "Nothing") is written in the words of the GM who wrote it; the starter set's pool and game names, dnd5e's rarity keys and coin abbreviations, compendium labels and item names are data, not words. `test/i18n.test.js` holds the file to the scripts: every key a script names is in it, every key in it is named, each call's data fills its sentence's placeholders, and no value is empty or an emoji.

## Making a crate from a roll table

A GM can turn a roll table into a crate in one step: **Make a crate** on the table's menu in the Roll Tables sidebar (right-click it), or drop the table on the manager's Boxes tab. The table's items go into a new pool named for it (store.js entriesFromDrop), and the crate editor opens on an unsaved draft named for the table, drawing from that pool, with the price focused: type a price and press **Add to shelf**. Cancel takes the new pool away again unless something else has come to use it. A table with no item results says so and makes nothing.

- **The table's odds.** The crate draws by entry weight alone (`rules.oddsMode: "weight"`), not by rarity band: a pool by its share, then an item by its weight, so a result that is 1 in 100 on the table stays 1 in 100. The editor shows the choice as **Odds: By rarity** or **Use the table's odds**, each with its sentence; on the table's odds the rarity weights are hidden (kept for a switch back). Pity and no-duplicates still work: what the character owns is left out, and a due pity keeps only the items at its rarity or better, by their weights. The strip's fillers follow the same weights and the near-miss tease is the same honest step up. A record with no `oddsMode` draws by rarity, exactly as before.
- **The shop names the real chances.** The card lists the best finds first by name with their chances ("Flame Tongue <1%"), up to four, then "and 3 more 56%", then "Nothing" when the crate can miss; the chances add up to 100 and never read 0%. The editor's preview names up to eight; the Boxes row says "the table's odds".
- **Nested tables are flattened.** A result that rolls another table brings in that table's items with the weights multiplied through (the result's weight times each item's share of its table), down to five tables deep; a table nested deeper, or one rolling a table above it, is skipped and counted. Weights stay whole numbers, scaled together so every ratio is exact.
- **Text results** ("You find nothing") are left out by default. The editor's **Count text results as nothing** (off for a new crate) makes their share of the table the share of pulls that give no item (`rules.missChance`): the core's miss, rolled before the item and never on a pull whose pity is due. The strip lands on a blank, the reveal says "Nothing this time." with the buyer's gold, the chat card says Nothing (sealed like any crate's until the spin lands), the pull counts toward pity, and the Pull log row refunds like any other.
- **Copied, not linked.** The editor says "Copied from <table> (N items, M nested tables flattened); edit the table and make the crate again to pick up changes." Editing the table later changes nothing until Make a crate is run again.

Dropping a roll table on a pool still adds its items to that pool after the preview, which now says which nested tables it flattened and how many text results it skipped.

## Stock and limits

Every editor field has a name of its own for a screen reader: each pool's checkbox is named for the pool and its share "Share of pulls from <pool>", off while the pool is unticked (an unticked pool is never drawn from, so its share does nothing); the pity count is "Pulls before the guarantee" ("Throws ..." at a well), the wheel's bounds "Smallest bet in gp" and "Largest bet in gp, blank for any", and a deck's card fields are numbered by the card's place ("Weight of card 2"). The shared rows live in `editorfields.js`, so a new field gets a name the same way. The Boxes and Pull log tables' button column is named "Actions" for a screen reader. Each box's editor has one **Limits** group. **Stock** is how many there are in all (blank for unlimited): the editor and the shop say what is left of it ("3 of 10 left"), each sale takes one, a refund puts one back (never past the GM's number), and **Restock** fills it again when you press Save (the line beside it says "10 of 10 left once you save"; Cancel leaves the stock as it was). Changing the number moves what is left by the same amount. **Each character may buy N in all, or N a day**: counted for the buyer's character from its unrefunded Pull log rows at that game (a day is the world's calendar, or a character's long rest with dnd5e's calendar off, `clock.js`, as for the well's cap), so a refund gives the purchase back and nothing is reset by hand. The GM checks it inside the write queue (`pull.js`), so two quick buys can't both pass, for every game that is pulled (the crate, the dice and the deck offer it in their editors); the shop card says "You can buy 1 more" and, once reached, "Limit reached: 2 of 2 for Crate Tester" with its Buy off. The record keeps `rules.stock` (left), `rules.stockMax` (the GM's number) and `rules.perCharacter` (`{ max, per: "ever" | "day" }`); a record from before them reads "3 left" and has no limit, as before. Every pull's log row now carries the in-game day it was bought on (`day`).

A refund from the Pull log undoes the whole pull: the coins come back, the granted item leaves the sheet, the box's stock goes up by one, and the character's pity count and no-duplicates record for that box go back to what they would be without that pull (the confirm dialog says where the count goes and that the item can drop again). Each log row keeps the pity count it was pulled at, whether the character already had the item, and whether the pull reset the count (`before.sinceRare`, `before.hadItem`, `resetPity`), so a refund frees the item even when an older row granted it before the record was reset by hand; a row written by 0.6.0 or earlier has none of these, so its refund corrects the no-duplicates record and the pull count and leaves the pity count as it is.

## The result waits for the spin

Everything that would tell a client the result waits until that client's spin lands (or is skipped or closed). The chat card shows "Opening…" in place of the item; a game whose card only some outcomes post (the dice's win, the well's find) has the whole message hidden until then, since the card turning up at all would tell. On a character sheet, the granted item's row stays hidden: anything that names it by `data-item-id`, in sheets already open and sheets opened mid-spin. In the shop, that crate's card keeps the character's pity line and odds from before the pull, then changes when the spin lands. Display only: nothing is written, and the item is on the sheet from the moment it is granted. A tab that is hidden, or set to reduced motion, lands at once, so nothing is held there. A replay holds nothing.

## Watch again

A pull's chat card carries a small display-only snapshot of its spin (`flags["loot-crates"].replay`: each card's name, art and rarity, where it lands, the odds and the result; no uuids, no purse, no granted item, about 6 KB). Anyone at the table can press **Watch again** on the card to replay that spin on their own screen, in a window titled "Replay: ...". A replay writes nothing, posts nothing, needs no GM and shows nobody's gold. Refunded cards and cards posted before this feature have no button.

A reveal that this client didn't see spin (it landed while the tab was hidden, the tab was hidden mid-spin, or Skip was pressed) also offers **Watch again** beside Close. It replays the same strip in the same window, display only, reading Skip while it plays; once a replay has run to its end the button goes. It never appears after a spin that played out, or with reduced motion on.

## Finished windows close by themselves

A reveal window (`reveal.js`, any kind's) and a table window (`tablewindow.js`) close by themselves once they are finished, after the delay each player picks in Game Settings (**Close finished game windows**, a client setting: never, 10 s, 30 s by default, or a minute). A reveal counts down once its result has landed (Skip counts; the chat card's seal has opened by then), and a Watch again inside it holds the countdown while it plays and starts it over once it lands. A table window counts down once its round is settled here with the result showing, or called off; never while bets are open or the stage plays, and not for a round this client hasn't heard of. The countdown pauses while the pointer is over the window, while the keyboard is at work in it, and while the page is hidden, then runs on with the time it had left. Focus the window puts on its own Close at the landing doesn't count as use. It closes the way the Close button does, so focus goes back where a Close sends it (the buyer's Buy, a replay's Watch again). A quiet line at the start of the actions row says "Closes in 30 s" (not a live region) with **Keep open**, which stops it for good. The countdown and the line are `linger.js` (pure); the shop, the manager, the welcome and dialogs never use it.

## What is atomic

Foundry gives a module no transaction and no compare-and-set: a world setting (the pools, the boxes, the Pull log, the pity state, the rounds) is written whole, an actor's coins are written as the new amounts, and the last write to reach the server wins. What the module guarantees on top of that, and what it does not:

- **One GM client writes one thing at a time.** Every read-modify-write on a GM's client goes through that client's one queue (`store.js` `enqueueWrite`): pulls, stakes, refunds, a late cancel's mark, the table's turns and gavel, the day's count, the missing-item sweep and the manager's edits to pools and games. A queued task reads the settings and the purse afresh when it starts and writes once it has decided, so two pulls, or a pull and a refund, on the same client never read the same purse, stock or log. (The shop switch is one value written whole, so it needs no queue.)
- **Every money move is one queued task.** A pull's charge, grant, stock, pity and log row, a stake's charge and its log row, and a refund's coins, item, stock, pity and mark are each decided and written inside one task, after its guards re-read what they check (the purse, the stock, the price pressed, the limits, the shelf). A failure after the charge puts the coins back in the same task (`pull.js`, `refund.js`).
- **A play's items are one creation and one refund.** A kind may grant several items for one play (`grants`); `pull.js` `createGrants` creates them in one `createEmbeddedDocuments` call, the row names them all (`itemIds`) and a refund removes every one still on the sheet together, naming each already gone. Another module's `preCreateItem` hook can cancel a creation without an error (Foundry returns fewer documents than asked): that is a failed grant, the same as a throw, so any item that was made is deleted again and the coins go back. Every grant is marked with Plutonium's own opt-out (`kinds.js` `readyToGrant`: `flags.plutonium.isStandardDragDrop` on an item Plutonium imported), so its "Use Importer?" prompt never cancels a grant or names the prize on the GM's screen before the seal lifts.
- **The queue is per client, not per world.** Two GMs' clients each have their own. Players' pulls and stakes go to the active GM (`game.users.activeGM`) and are taken by whichever GM is active when the request arrives (`socket.js` `isActiveGM`), so one play never runs on two clients. A table's turn and its gavel check again inside the queue, just before they write (`table.js` `tableTick`, `resolveRound`), so a GM who has handed over writes nothing more for a round.

The window that is left: two GM clients writing at the same moment. That happens when a second GM's manager action (an edit, a price, a refund) lands while the active GM writes a pull, or when the active GM hands over (another GM joins or the first leaves) while the old GM's queue still holds a pull or stake it took before: that task runs to the end on the old GM, beside the new GM's queue. Each write re-reads just before it writes, so the window is about one settings round trip (milliseconds on a LAN), and the active-GM checks above keep any one play to one client. Inside that window the later write replaces the earlier one's change to the same setting or the same purse: a Pull log row, a price edit or, if the two touch the same character, a purse change can be lost. Closing it needs a server-side atomic write (compare-and-set or a transaction), which Foundry does not offer, or every GM write sent to the active GM over the socket.

## Layout

`module/` is what ships: `module.json`, `lang/en.json` (every word, by key), `styles/module.css` and `scripts/`. `scripts/main.js` is the entry point Foundry loads: the settings, the hooks and the module API. The rest sit in four folders. `games/`, `core/` and `views/` are pure: no Foundry globals, so Vitest and the harness run them, and lint enforces it. `foundry/` is everything that talks to Foundry.
